What the Garden State Plug-In Solar Act does (the plug-in solar part)

The Act (S2368/A4836) creates a clear state-level path for plug-in / balcony solar in New Jersey. Verified terms:

  • Cap: 1,200 watts of panel capacity for a plug-in solar system.
  • Certified equipment. The device must be certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory — in practice a UL listing or equivalent.
  • Passed unanimously: Senate 40-0 and Assembly 79-0 on June 30, 2026.
  • Effective date: [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]] (six months after enactment).

For the exact conditions — including any notification or fee provisions — see the enacted text, [[CHAPTER_OR_LAW_REF]] (linked in Sources below). This puts New Jersey alongside Utah, Maine, Virginia, Colorado, Maryland, Connecticut, Vermont, and New Hampshire.

Why New Jersey matters: it is one of the most renter- and apartment-dense states in the country — the Jersey City / Newark commuter belt alone is hundreds of thousands of rental units — and residential electricity runs above the national average (~18¢/kWh). A plug-in kit is one of the only solar options open to a renter, so a statewide plug-in solar law reaches a very large audience here.

What you can do now (before [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]])

You don’t have to wait until [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]] to install plug-in solar — you just do it under your utility’s existing interconnection rules until then. New Jersey’s main residential utilities:

  1. PSE&G (largest, most of the state)
  2. JCP&L (Jersey Central Power & Light)

What to ask (and get in writing):

  • Maximum wattage allowed behind-the-meter without a permit before [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]].
  • Whether a one-page interconnection notification is required.
  • Whether they require a specific microinverter UL-listing (UL 1741 / UL 3700).

From [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]] onwards: the 1,200W cap under the Garden State Plug-In Solar Act applies statewide for compliant, certified kits.
