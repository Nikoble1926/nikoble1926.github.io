What the SUNNY Act actually does (the plug-in solar part)

The SUNNY Act (A.9111C / S.8512C) carves out a clear state-level path for plug-in / balcony solar in New York. Key terms:

  • Cap: [[CAP_W]] watts of panel capacity per household (one plug-in solar device per customer).
  • UL-certified equipment. The microinverter (and panels where required) must be certified by an accredited testing laboratory — in practice this means a UL listing or equivalent.
  • No utility approval, no interconnection agreement, no fees for systems within the cap. This removes the case-by-case utility gatekeeping that previously governed plug-in solar in New York.
  • Effective date: [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]].

This puts New York on the same legal footing as Utah, Maine, Virginia, Colorado, Maryland, and Connecticut — with New York now the largest U.S. state by population to legalize plug-in solar at the state level.

What you can do now (before [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]])

You don’t have to wait until [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]] to install plug-in solar — you just have to do it under your utility’s existing interconnection rules until then. New York’s major investor-owned utilities:

  5. Orange & Rockland

What to ask (and get in writing):

  • Maximum wattage allowed behind-the-meter without a permit before [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]].
  • Whether a one-page interconnection notification is required.
  • Whether they require a specific microinverter UL-listing (UL 1741 / UL 3700).

From [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]] onwards: the [[CAP_W]]W cap under the SUNNY Act applies statewide; no utility paperwork is required for compliant kits.
