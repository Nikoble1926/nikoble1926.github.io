Quick tip: Gov. [[GOVERNOR]] signed SB 540 on [[SIGN_DATE]]. New Hampshire
becomes the [[STATE_ORDINAL]] U.S. state to legalize plug-in / balcony solar at
the state level.

The plug-in part:
 - Cap [[CAP_W]]W of panel capacity per household, one device per customer.
 - No utility approval, no interconnection paperwork, no fees within the cap.
 - Equipment must be certified by an accredited testing laboratory (UL or
   equivalent — i.e. UL 1741 / UL 3700).
 - Provisions take effect [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]].

Why it matters beyond NH: New Hampshire has some of the highest residential
electricity rates in the U.S. (~24¢/kWh) and a large concentration of
rate-sensitive renters in Manchester, Nashua, and the Seacoast. SB 540 makes
plug-in solar payback ~3 years on an 800W kit (vs. 5–7 in lower-rate states),
and adds momentum to the Northeast movement that started with Connecticut's HB
5340 on June 4, 2026.
