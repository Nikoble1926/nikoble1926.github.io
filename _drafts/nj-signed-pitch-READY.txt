Quick tip: Gov. Sherrill signed the Garden State Plug-In Solar Act
(S2368/A4836) on [[SIGN_DATE]]. New Jersey becomes the 9th U.S. state to
legalize plug-in / balcony solar at the state level.

The plug-in part (verified from the bill):
 - Cap: a maximum power output of not more than 1,200 watts - output, not panel capacity.
 - Equipment must be certified by a nationally recognized testing
   laboratory (UL or equivalent — i.e. UL 1741 / UL 3700).
 - Passed both chambers unanimously on June 30, 2026 (Senate 40-0,
   Assembly 79-0) — a rare fully bipartisan energy vote.
 - Provisions take effect [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]] (six months after enactment).
 - Enacted citation: [[CHAPTER_OR_LAW_REF]].

Why it matters beyond NJ: New Jersey is one of the most renter- and
apartment-dense states in the country — the Jersey City / Newark commuter
belt alone is hundreds of thousands of rental units — and residential
electricity runs above the national average (~18¢/kWh). Plug-in solar is
one of the only solar options open to a renter, so a statewide law here
reaches an unusually large audience. It also extends the Northeast wave
that ran through Connecticut (HB 5340, June 4), Vermont (S.202, June 16)
and New Hampshire (SB 540, July 2).
