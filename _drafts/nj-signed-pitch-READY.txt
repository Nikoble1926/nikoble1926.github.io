Quick tip: Gov. Sherrill signed the Garden State Balcony Solar Act
(S2368/A4836) on September 1, 2026. New Jersey is the 9th U.S. state to pass
a plug-in solar law - but the plug-in provisions do not take effect until
March 1, 2027, six months after enactment. Until then New Jersey stays under
ordinary utility interconnection rules, so "legal in New Jersey today" would
be wrong and we do not write it that way.

The plug-in part, read from the bill text (third reprint):
 - Cap: a maximum power output of not more than 1,200 watts. The Act measures
   output - what the device sends out - not the rated capacity of the panels
   behind it.
 - Certified equipment: the device must be "listed or certified in accordance
   with Underwriters Laboratories Standard No. 3700... or a comparable
   standard, or a combination of standards" by a nationally recognized testing
   laboratory.
 - It must stop exporting in a blackout.
 - 1,200W is also a per-address cap: the combined output of all such devices
   at one service address, or one dwelling unit in multi-family housing.
 - Landlords and homeowners' / condominium associations may not prohibit it,
   though reasonable restrictions on size and placement are allowed.
 - A tenant must give at least 14 days' written notice.
 - No municipal permit, application, fee or licence may be required.
 - Provisions take effect March 1, 2027.

We are not quoting an enacted chapter citation. One was circulating; we could
not verify it from njleg and so it does not appear on our page either.

Why it matters beyond NJ: New Jersey is one of the most renter- and
apartment-dense states in the country - the Jersey City / Newark commuter
belt alone is hundreds of thousands of rental units - and residential
electricity runs above the national average (~18c/kWh). Plug-in solar is one
of the only solar options open to a renter, so a statewide law here reaches
an unusually large audience. It also extends the Northeast wave that ran
through Connecticut (HB 5340, June 4), Vermont (S.202, June 16) and New
Hampshire (SB 540, July 2). New York's SUNNY Act is still with Gov. Hochul;
if she signs, New York is the 10th.
