Two different jobs. Make sure you want the right one.

A grid-tie kit feeds power into your home while the sun is up, which lowers what you draw from the utility. It shuts down in a blackout by design, so that line workers are not exposed to your power.

A power station does the opposite. It will run your fridge in an outage and it lowers nothing on an ordinary day.

Most people who arrive on this page want the first one. If you want backup as well, that is a second purchase, not a bigger version of the first.

The bill: HB 5764

Michigan has a plug-in solar bill in play, but it has not moved since spring. HB 5764 would amend the state’s 2008 energy law (2008 PA 295) by adding a section on small portable solar generation devices.

  • 19 March 2026 — introduced by Rep. Will Snyder, read a first time and referred to the House Committee on Energy.
  • 14 April 2026 — the last action recorded on the official page.
  • 15 sponsors from both parties, which is unusual for an energy bill in Michigan.
  • It has not been reported out of committee, and it is not law.

Source: Michigan Legislature — House Bill 5764 of 2026. Read 23 August 2026. We track the status page, not the bill text.

Before you buy in Michigan

Call your electric utility and ask: (1) do they allow small plug-in / behind-the-meter grid-tied solar, and (2) is any notification or interconnection form required? Stay within the wattage they specify, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
