Is Plug-In Solar Legal in New Jersey?

Signed — takes effect 1 Mar 2027   Avg rate ~18¢/kWh   Updated: September 11, 2026

Quick answer: New Jersey has signed a plug-in solar law — the Garden State Balcony Solar Act (S2368/A4836), signed by Gov. What the Garden State Balcony Solar Act does (the plug-in solar part)

The Act (S2368/A4836) creates a clear state-level path for plug-in / balcony solar in New Jersey. Verified terms:

  • Cap: 1,200 watts of panel capacity for a plug-in solar system.
  • Certified equipment. The Act’s own wording: a device must be “listed or certified in accordance with Underwriters Laboratories Standard No. 3700… or a comparable standard, or a combination of standards” by a nationally recognized testing laboratory.
  • It must stop exporting in a blackout. The definition requires a device or feature that prevents export to the distribution system during an outage.
  • 1,200W is also a per-address cap. The combined output of all such devices at one service address — or one dwelling unit in multi-family housing — may not exceed 1,200W without an interconnection agreement.
  • Landlords and HOAs cannot ban it. A landlord may not prohibit a tenant who pays their own electric bill from using one within the leased premises, including a leased balcony or patio; reasonable restrictions on size and placement are allowed. Homeowners’ and condominium associations may not enforce a prohibition either.
  • 14 days’ written notice. A tenant must notify the landlord or association at least 14 days before placing or using the device, with documentation that it meets the definition.
  • No municipal permit or fee. A municipality may not prohibit use on a residential property, nor require a permit, application, fee or licence for it — but building or electrical work is still subject to the usual codes and inspections.
  • Passed both chambers on June 30, 2026.
  • Effective date: March 1, 2027 (six months after enactment).

For the exact conditions — including any notification or fee provisions — see the bill text (linked in Sources below). This puts New Jersey alongside Utah, Maine, Virginia, Colorado, Maryland, Connecticut, Vermont, and New Hampshire.

Why New Jersey matters: it is one of the most renter- and apartment-dense states in the country — the Jersey City / Newark commuter belt alone is hundreds of thousands of rental units — and residential electricity runs above the national average (~18¢/kWh). A plug-in kit is one of the only solar options open to a renter, so a statewide plug-in solar law reaches a very large audience here.

What you can do now (before March 1, 2027)

You don’t have to wait until March 1, 2027 to install plug-in solar — you just do it under your utility’s existing interconnection rules until then. New Jersey’s main residential utilities:

  1. PSE&G (largest, most of the state)
  2. JCP&L (Jersey Central Power & Light)

What to ask (and get in writing):

  • Maximum wattage allowed behind-the-meter without a permit before March 1, 2027.
  • Whether a one-page interconnection notification is required.
  • Which microinverter certification they will accept. That is the utility’s own requirement for the route you would use today — it is not what the Act says.

From March 1, 2027 onwards: the 1,200W cap under the Garden State Balcony Solar Act applies statewide, and the certification standard that matters is the one the Act itself names — UL 3700 or a comparable standard, quoted in full above — rather than whatever your utility asks for today.

FAQ

Is balcony solar legal in New Jersey now?
Not yet under the new law. Gov. Sherrill signed the Garden State Balcony Solar Act (S2368/A4836) on September 1, 2026, but its plug-in / balcony solar provisions do not take effect until March 1, 2027 — six months after enactment. Until then your utility’s existing interconnection rules apply. From that date a plug-in solar system up to 1,200W is allowed, provided the device is “listed or certified in accordance with Underwriters Laboratories Standard No. 3700… or a comparable standard” by a nationally recognized testing laboratory.
What does the Garden State Balcony Solar Act allow?
It establishes a state-level allowance for plug-in (balcony) solar up to 1,200W of panel capacity, using equipment certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory. The bill passed both chambers on June 30, 2026. For the exact conditions, see the bill text.
When does the plug-in solar provision take effect?
March 1, 2027 — six months after enactment. The Act was signed September 1, 2026 but the plug-in solar provisions are not in force until then.
What can I do before March 1, 2027?
You can still install a plug-in solar kit today, but until March 1, 2027 you do it under your utility’s existing interconnection rules. In New Jersey, call PSE&G or JCP&L, ask what wattage they allow behind-the-meter, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
