Is Plug-In Solar Legal in Maine?

Legal — signed (eff. Jul 15, 2026)   Avg rate ~24¢/kWh

Last verified: June 14, 2026 · source data

Quick answer: Plug-in / balcony solar is legal in Maine under LD 1730, signed on April 6, 2026. Systems up to 1,200W of export capacity are allowed without utility approval, interconnection paperwork, or fees, provided the equipment is certified by an accredited testing laboratory (UL or equivalent), effective July 15, 2026. Maine now has a plug-in solar law. LD 1730, signed by Gov. Janet Mills on April 6, 2026, takes effect July 15, 2026. The 420W split is real. Under 35-A MRSA §3475(3) you may install and operate systems with a combined inverter output of up to 420 watts AC per service address yourself. Above 420W and up to 1,200W, each system must be installed by an electrician licensed in Maine and must use a dedicated circuit with a single outlet — and you must notify your utility, on a form prescribed by the Commission, within 30 days of installation (§3475(5)). The utility cannot refuse a compliant system. Note also §3475(4): an eligible system may not be used for net energy billing, so exported power earns you nothing — size it to what you actually use. The statute requires UL or an equivalent nationally recognized testing laboratory. Because electricity here is among the most expensive in the country, even a small plug-in kit can pay for itself unusually fast — one of the best states in the U.S. for balcony solar economics. Residential power mostly comes from Central Maine Power and Versant; before buying, confirm their interconnection or notification steps, stay within the wattage rules, and choose a UL-listed kit.

Before you buy in Maine

Call your electric utility and ask: (1) do they allow small plug-in / behind-the-meter grid-tied solar, and (2) is any notification or interconnection form required? Stay within the wattage they specify, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
