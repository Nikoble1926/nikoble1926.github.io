Two different jobs. Make sure you want the right one.

A grid-tie kit feeds power into your home while the sun is up, which lowers what you draw from the utility. It shuts down in a blackout by design, so that line workers are not exposed to your power.

A power station does the opposite. It will run your fridge in an outage and it lowers nothing on an ordinary day.

Most people who arrive on this page want the first one. If you want backup as well, that is a second purchase, not a bigger version of the first.

The bill: SB 3104 and the 391-watt line

Illinois has the most developed plug-in solar bill of any state that has not passed one. SB 3104, filed by Sen. Rachel Ventura and carrying 17 Senate sponsors, got further than most and then ran out of calendar.

  • 29 January 2026 — filed and read a first time.
  • 12 March 2026 — reported Do Pass as Amended by Energy and Public Utilities, 9–4.
  • 25 March 2026 — passed second reading and was placed on the third-reading calendar.
  • The third-reading deadline was extended three times — 8 May, 15 May, 22 May.
  • 22 May 2026 — re-referred to Assignments under Senate Rule 3-9(a). That is where it stopped: it never got a third reading, so the full Senate never voted.

What the amended bill would do

Senate Committee Amendment No. 2, adopted 12 March 2026, replaced the bill’s text. Its central number is one no other state uses:

  • 391 watts or less to a receptacle outlet — exempt from building safety code or product listing provisions that would require alterations to the building’s premises, wiring or electrical panels.
  • More than 391 watts — installation prohibited until the National Fire Protection Association adopts a National Electrical Code standard covering those systems.
  • Certification by Underwriters Laboratories or an equivalent nationally recognized testing laboratory is required.
  • Systems manufactured on or after 1 January 2027 need a label; selling one without it would be an unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act.
  • The owner must notify the electricity provider within 30 days of installation.
  • No net metering and no distributed generation rebate.
  • Homeowners’ associations, condominium associations and landlords could not prohibit a plug-in solar system.

Source: Illinois General Assembly — Bill Status of SB3104, including the official synopsis of Senate Committee Amendment No. 2. Read 23 August 2026. None of this is in force.

Before you buy in Illinois

Call your electric utility and ask: (1) do they allow small plug-in / behind-the-meter grid-tied solar, and (2) is any notification or interconnection form required? Stay within the wattage they specify, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
