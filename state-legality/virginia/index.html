Is Plug-In Solar Legal in Virginia?

Signed — takes effect 1 Jan 2027   Avg rate ~14¢/kWh

Last verified: August 1, 2026 · source data

Quick answer: Plug-in / balcony solar will be legal in Virginia from 1 January 2027 under HB 395 / SB 250, signed 22 April 2026 as Chapters 1052 and 998. Until then your utility’s existing rules apply. From that date a device supplying up to 1,200W of power output needs no utility approval and no fee — but you must notify your utility on a State Corporation Commission form before installing, and it has 15 days to review. A device up to 391W is additionally exempt from product-listing rules that would require changes to the building’s wiring. Equipment must be certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory. Average residential rate is around 14¢/kWh.

Virginia has a plug-in solar law, but it is not in force yet. HB 395 and SB 250 — identical bills, enacted as Chapters 1052 and 998 — were approved by the Governor on 22 April 2026. Every section they create carries a delayed commencement: § 56-596.8 (exemption from interconnection), § 55.1-1212.1 (tenants) and § 15.2-2288.7 (local regulation) all take effect 1 January 2027. Until then Dominion Energy’s existing interconnection policy applies to you.

From 1 January 2027 the statutory definition caps a device at 1,200W of power output per customer — per residential building unit in multi-family housing. A device whose maximum power output to the receptacle outlet is 391W or less is additionally exempt from product-listing provisions that would require alterations to the building’s premises, wiring or electrical panels.

The law does not remove all paperwork. You must notify your electric service provider before installing, using a form published by the State Corporation Commission, online or by mail; the provider then has 15 days to review it and raise any deficiency. If it does not respond within that period you are deemed to have met the requirement. Where more than one device sits at a single property, the utility may install an automatic locking disconnect switch.

Two limits worth knowing. A landlord who owns more than four rental units cannot prohibit a tenant from installing a device on the exterior of the tenant’s premises, subject to reasonable restrictions and seven days’ notice. But § 15.2-2288.7(F) expressly preserves recorded covenants, condominium instruments and property owners’ association declarations — Virginia does not override your HOA. And no provision applies to a rental unit on a ratio utility billing system.

Before you buy in Virginia

Call your electric utility and ask: (1) do they allow small plug-in / behind-the-meter grid-tied solar, and (2) is any notification or interconnection form required? Stay within the wattage they specify, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
