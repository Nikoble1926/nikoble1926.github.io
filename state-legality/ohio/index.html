Two different jobs. Make sure you want the right one.

A grid-tie kit feeds power into your home while the sun is up, which lowers what you draw from the utility. It shuts down in a blackout by design, so that line workers are not exposed to your power.

A power station does the opposite. It will run your fridge in an outage and it lowers nothing on an ordinary day.

Most people who arrive on this page want the first one. If you want backup as well, that is a second purchase, not a bigger version of the first.

The bill: HB 755

HB 755, titled “Regards portable solar generation devices”, would amend section 4928.01 and enact sections 4928.74, 4928.741 and 4928.742 of the Revised Code. Its primary sponsors are Tristan Rader and Chris Glassburn.

The official status tracker shows exactly two steps completed:

  • Introduced in House — done.
  • In House Committee (House Energy Committee) — done.
  • Reported by Committee — not reached. Everything after it — passage by the House, introduction in the Senate, the Governor — is still ahead.

One correction worth stating plainly. Secondary coverage has described this bill as sitting in the Senate Energy Committee. The legislature’s own page shows it has not yet been reported out of the House committee. We follow the official page.

Source: Ohio Legislature — House Bill 755, 136th General Assembly. Read 23 August 2026.

Before you buy in Ohio

Call your electric utility and ask: (1) do they allow small plug-in / behind-the-meter grid-tied solar, and (2) is any notification or interconnection form required? Stay within the wattage they specify, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
