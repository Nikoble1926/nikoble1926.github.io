Is Plug-In Solar Legal in Colorado?

Legal — signed (eff. 12 Aug 2026)   Avg rate ~15¢/kWh

Last verified: August 1, 2026 · source data

Quick answer: Plug-in / balcony solar is legal in Colorado under HB26-1007, signed 7 May 2026. The act takes effect 12 August 2026: a device supplying up to 1,920W to the grid needs no utility approval, no interconnection paperwork and no fee, provided it is labelled and listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory. A device up to 391W is additionally exempt from the state's solar PV installation requirements. Two parts of the act are delayed to 1 January 2027 — the UL rule for sales above 391W, and the protections against HOA covenants. Average residential rate is around 15¢/kWh.

Colorado now has a plug-in solar law. HB26-1007, signed by Gov. Jared Polis on 7 May 2026, takes effect 12 August 2026 — ninety days after the general assembly adjourned — and creates a formal legal category for “portable-scale solar generation devices.” The statutory definition caps a device at 1,920W of output to the electric grid, not panel nameplate, which is the highest plug-in cap in the U.S. From that date a utility cannot require its approval, charge a fee, or demand extra equipment, and the act also requires utilities to accept meter-collar adapters.

There is a second, lighter tier: a device of 391W or less is exempt from the state's solar PV installation requirements and from building-code provisions that would require changes to the building's wiring.

Two parts of the act are delayed to 1 January 2027. From that date it becomes unlawful to sell, lease or rent a device above 391W that is not UL labelled and listed; and from that date a covenant that unreasonably prohibits a device is void, though a property owner may still impose reasonable restrictions such as prior notice, fire and overload precautions, and where the device is placed. Those HOA protections are not in force yet. Confirm the setup and any interconnection steps with Xcel Energy or your co-op before you buy.

Before you buy in Colorado

Call your electric utility and ask: (1) do they allow small plug-in / behind-the-meter grid-tied solar, and (2) is any notification or interconnection form required? Stay within the wattage they specify, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
