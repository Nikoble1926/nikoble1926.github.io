Is Plug-In Solar Legal in New Hampshire?

Signed — takes effect 1 Jan 2027   Avg rate ~24¢/kWh   Updated: July 2, 2026 · Last verified: August 1, 2026

Quick answer: Plug-in / balcony solar will be legal in New Hampshire from 1 January 2027 under SB 540, signed 2 July 2026 as Chapter 262. Until then your utility’s existing rules apply. From that date each metered customer is allowed one system with a maximum inverter output of 1,200 watts AC, with no utility approval, no interconnection review and no fee — though your utility may ask you to report the system’s wattage after installation, for notification only. Equipment must be certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory. Check your insurance: the act requires the state to warn that connecting an unlisted system, or one installed against the building code or manufacturer instructions, may affect your homeowners or renters cover. Average residential rate is around 24¢/kWh.

✅ SB 540 signed into law July 2, 2026. Gov. Kelly Ayotte signed New Hampshire’s plug-in solar bill (SB 540) on July 2, 2026. The plug-in / balcony solar provisions allow any utility customer to use one plug-in solar device up to 1,200W per household, with no utility approval, no interconnection paperwork, and no fees — provided the device is certified by an accredited testing laboratory (UL or equivalent). The provisions take effect January 1, 2027.

What SB 540 actually does (the plug-in solar part)

SB 540 creates a clear state-level path for plug-in / balcony solar in New Hampshire. Key terms:

  • Cap: 1,200 watts AC inverter output — one plug-in solar generation system per metered customer.
  • UL-certified equipment. The microinverter (and panels where required) must be certified by an accredited testing laboratory — in practice this means a UL listing or equivalent.
  • No utility approval, no interconnection agreement, no fees for systems within the cap.
  • Effective date: January 1, 2027.

That puts New Hampshire among the states with a dedicated plug-in solar law — though the caps, the commencement dates and even what the watts are measured at differ from state to state: Utah, Maine, Virginia, Colorado, Maryland, Connecticut, and Vermont.

National context: NH is the 8th state to sign

New Hampshire is the 8th U.S. state to sign a plug-in solar law. The signed-into-law list now (chronological):

  1. Utah — HB 340 (March 2025) — first ever
  2. Maine — LD 1730 (April 6, 2026)
  3. Virginia — HB 395 / SB 250 (April 22, 2026)
  4. Colorado — HB26-1007 (May 7, 2026) — highest cap (1,920W)
  5. Maryland — HB 1532 (May 12, 2026)
  6. Connecticut — HB 5340 (June 4, 2026)
  7. Vermont — S.202 (June 16, 2026)
  8. New Hampshire — SB 540 (July 2, 2026)

New York (SUNNY Act, A.9111C / S.8512C) and New Jersey (Garden State Plug-In Solar Act, S2368/A4836 — passed both chambers unanimously on June 30, 2026) have also passed their legislatures and are awaiting their Governors’ signatures.

One part of the act has no date at all. Section 3 inserts RSA 155-A:16, which directs the building code review board to amend the state building code to cover buildings using a plug-in system. Under 262:6 it takes effect only when the commissioner of the Department of Energy certifies to the Office of Legislative Services and the Secretary of State that a nationally recognized standard exists authorising plug-in solar systems to be safely connected to a building’s electrical system. There is no deadline for that certification. Meanwhile RSA 362-A:10 still requires every system to be installed and operated in accordance with the state building code — the code that section 3 is meant to update.

Insurance is the one to watch. The act obliges the Department of Energy to publish notice that connecting an unlisted system, or connecting one in a manner not in accordance with the state building code or manufacturer instructions, may affect your homeowners or renters insurance coverage. That is a bigger practical risk for most people than anything the utility can do.

Why New Hampshire matters: among the highest residential electricity rates in the U.S. (~24¢/kWh) — meaning an 800W kit pays back in ~3 years vs. 5–7 years in lower-rate states. SB 540 makes plug-in solar a default for any NH renter or homeowner.

What you can do now (before January 1, 2027)

You don’t have to wait until January 1, 2027 to install plug-in solar — you just have to do it under your utility’s existing interconnection rules until then. New Hampshire’s major investor-owned utilities and co-ops:

  1. Eversource NH (largest, most of the state)
  2. Unitil (Seacoast, Capital area)
  3. NH Electric Cooperative (rural northern / western NH)
  4. Liberty Utilities (small footprint)

What to ask (and get in writing):

  • Maximum wattage allowed behind-the-meter without a permit before January 1, 2027.
  • Whether a one-page interconnection notification is required.
  • Whether they require a specific microinverter UL-listing (UL 1741 / UL 3700).

From January 1, 2027 onwards: the 1,200W cap under SB 540 applies statewide. No approval, no interconnection review and no fee — but RSA 362-A:10, II lets your utility set up a way for you to report the system’s wattage after you install it, for notification only.

FAQ

Is balcony solar legal in New Hampshire now?
Signed, but not in force yet. Gov. Kelly Ayotte signed SB 540 on July 2, 2026. The plug-in / balcony solar provisions take effect January 1, 2027. From the effective date, a single plug-in solar device up to 1,200W is legal without utility approval, interconnection paperwork, or fees.
What does SB 540 actually allow?
It allows any utility customer to use one plug-in solar device with a maximum inverter output of 1,200 watts AC per metered customer. The device must be certified by an accredited testing laboratory (UL or equivalent). No utility approval, no interconnection agreement, and no fees are required for systems within the cap.
When does the plug-in solar part take effect?
January 1, 2027. The bill was signed July 2, 2026 but the balcony solar provisions are not in force until then.
What can I do before January 1, 2027?
You can still install a plug-in solar kit today, but until January 1, 2027 you have to do it under your utility’s existing interconnection rules — not under SB 540. Call Eversource NH, Unitil, NH Electric Cooperative, or Liberty Utilities, ask what wattage they allow behind-the-meter, use a UL-listed microinverter, and keep written confirmation.
