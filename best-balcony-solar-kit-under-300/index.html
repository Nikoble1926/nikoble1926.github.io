What “under $300” actually buys

Plug-in solar has two cost pieces: the grid-tie microinverter (the safety brain that turns panel DC into household AC) and the panel(s). A complete boxed kit bundles both — convenient, but the bundled kits we track (like the AUECOOR 600W kit) sit in the premium tier and usually run above $300.

So under $300 the realistic move is microinverter-first: buy a budget grid-tie microinverter (often the cheapest single component) and pair it with one 400–500W panel you supply. You get a working setup now and can add panels later. Below are the lowest-cost, in-stock microinverters we’ve reviewed.

FAQ

Can you really get balcony solar for under $300?
Yes — usually as a microinverter plus one budget panel rather than a boxed all-in-one kit. A grid-tie microinverter is the cheapest entry point; a complete kit with panels included typically costs more than $300. Check live prices before buying.
What is the cheapest way to start plug-in solar?
A budget grid-tie microinverter (often the lowest-cost component) paired with a single panel you already own or buy separately. It costs less than a complete kit and lets you expand later.
Is a cheap microinverter safe?
Look for a UL-listed (UL 1741 / UL 3700) grid-tie microinverter with built-in anti-islanding, which shuts the unit off if the grid goes down. Avoid no-name units with no listed certification. Confirm what your utility allows on our state legality tracker.
