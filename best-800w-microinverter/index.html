What makes a good 800W microinverter

Ignore the marketing and check four things: a UL listing (UL 1741 / UL 3700) with anti-islanding (the unit disconnects in a fraction of a second if the grid drops, protecting line workers); conversion efficiency (good units land ~96–97%); monitoring (a WiFi app or at least an on-unit LCD); and voltage compatibility with your outlet (120V in North America, though some units handle 120/230V). The 800W ceiling mirrors Europe’s balcony-solar rules — U.S. rules are still forming, so confirm what your utility allows on our state legality tracker.

FAQ

Why 800W for a plug-in solar microinverter?
800W is the common cap many plug-in / balcony inverters follow, mirroring Europe’s balcony-solar rules. It is enough to meaningfully cut a household bill while staying within what most utilities treat as a small behind-the-meter system. U.S. rules are still forming — confirm what your utility allows on our state tracker.
What specs matter most?
A UL listing (UL 1741 / UL 3700) and built-in anti-islanding, conversion efficiency (~96–97% on good units), monitoring (WiFi app or on-unit LCD), and voltage compatibility with your outlet (120V in North America; some units do 120/230V).
Can I run an 800W microinverter on a normal outlet?
Technically yes via the inverter’s plug, but legality and the exact connection depend on your utility and state. Use a UL-listed unit with anti-islanding and check our state legality tracker first.
Do I need 800W, or is 600W enough?
600W is cheaper and fine for one or two panels and a smaller offset. 800W gives more headroom for a second panel and slightly higher output. If you may expand, the small step up is usually worth it — see the cheapest setups under $300.
