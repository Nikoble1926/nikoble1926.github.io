The one thing most comparisons get wrong

Watt-hours and watts are not the same specification, and here they point in opposite directions.

Watt-hours (Wh) = how long. The S2000 stores 2,010Wh against the C1000’s 1,024Wh — roughly double the energy, so roughly double the runtime on the same load.

Watts (W) = what it can run at once. Here the smaller unit wins: the C1000 is rated 2,000W with a 3,000W peak, while the S2000 is rated 1,500W. If an appliance needs more than 1,500W to start, the cheaper C1000 is the one more likely to handle it.

So the honest framing is not “which is better” but which failure worries you: running out of charge, or not being able to start something.

FAQ

Should I buy the Anker SOLIX C1000 or the S2000?
Buy the C1000 Gen 2 at $499.98 if your worry is a normal outage of a few hours to a day, or if you need more starting power — it is rated 2,000W with a 3,000W peak. Buy the S2000 at $649.99 if you need to run essentials for longer: it carries 2,010Wh, roughly double the energy, and Anker states up to 35 hours of fridge backup. The important catch is that the S2000 is rated 1,500W, which is lower than the C1000. More energy does not mean more power.
Why does the bigger S2000 have a lower wattage than the C1000?
Because capacity and output are two different specifications. Capacity, measured in watt-hours, is how much energy is stored — how long it lasts. Output, measured in watts, is how much can be drawn at once — what it can run. The S2000 stores about twice as much (2,010Wh vs 1,024Wh) but is rated to deliver 1,500W, against the C1000’s 2,000W with a 3,000W peak. So the S2000 runs your fridge far longer, while the C1000 is the one more likely to start a demanding appliance.
How much more is the S2000?
Read on 2 September 2026, the S2000 was $649.99 and the C1000 Gen 2 was $499.98 — a difference of $150.01. On 25 August the same two listings were $699.99 and $499.98, a $200 difference, so the gap has narrowed by $49.99 in eight days. Prices in this category move frequently, so confirm both before ordering.
Which one is better for hurricane season?
For a multi-day outage the S2000 is the better base, because runtime is what runs out first — Anker states up to 35 hours of fridge backup from its 2,010Wh. For a shorter outage where you need to start heavier equipment, the C1000’s 2,000W rating and 3,000W peak give more headroom. If budget allows only one and you live somewhere storms knock power out for days rather than hours, size wins.
Is the S2000 the same as the Anker C2000 Gen 2?
No, and this is the most common confusion in Anker’s range. Anker sells two different 2kWh-class stations in the US. The S2000 holds 2,010Wh and is rated 1,500W — it is built for runtime. The C2000 Gen 2 holds 2,048Wh and is rated 2,400W with a 4,000W peak, and it expands to 4kWh with an expansion battery. Checked 25 August 2026, the C2000 Gen 2 was $949.99 against the S2000’s $649.99. If you want a larger battery and also want more power than the C1000 Gen 2 gives you, the C2000 Gen 2 is the model you want, not the S2000. We compare it directly in C1000 vs C2000 Gen 2.
