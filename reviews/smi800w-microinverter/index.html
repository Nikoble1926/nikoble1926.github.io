The SMI800W is a low-cost 800 W WiFi microinverter for buyers who already know how to match panels to an inverter. Because no US certification is stated on its listing, it is not where we would send a first-time buyer — see plug-in solar with UL listings first. It does exactly what a balcony-solar brain should: convert your panel's power to household AC, feed it through a normal outlet, and show you live production in a clean WiFi app.

Output & monitoring

Rated 800W with 96% peak efficiency (listing) and pure sine output. The WiFi app shows real-time and historical production so you can tune your panel angle for more.

Who it's for

Buyers who already have (or will choose) their own panels and want an app-connected inverter.

Verdict

A low-cost WiFi microinverter whose listing gives input figures, but with no US certification stated and no manufacturer datasheet we could find. If you would rather buy everything in one box, see complete kits.

What one owner reported

A reader (credited at their request as “Happy Reader”) runs four 200 W bifacial N-type TOPCon panels (Callsun) on a ground mount tilted about 29°, facing south-east, with tree shade from about 4 p.m. We have not independently verified any of these figures.

  • 16 August 2026, two panels in parallel on each input (2P+2P): the unit “usually outputs only 600W-ish” (AC output).
  • 17 August 2026, two panels in series on each input (2S+2S), about 2:23 p.m. local time at 102 °F: “45v @ 13.6amps”, and 738 W a little earlier (DC input from the panels).
  • Reported 7 October 2026, 2S+2S, on a “very clear” day with white-painted boards under the bifacial panels: 889 W (DC input — “All figures I send are generated watts, not converted watts”). The AC output at that moment was not reported.
  • 15 October 2026: “Barely 30w to 60w due to heavy clouds and rain” (the reader did not say whether this was input or output).
Series wiring warning: Two of these panels in series give about 54.6 V open-circuit at 25 °C. With Callsun’s −0.25 %/°C coefficient that reaches 60 V at about −14 °C (6 °F) on a cold, clear morning. The 60 V figure is the top of the 16–60 V input range stated on the Amazon listing (checked 28 October 2026) and shown on the unit’s own display; we have not found it in a manufacturer datasheet. Do not wire panels in series to this inverter until the maker confirms its maximum DC input voltage.
